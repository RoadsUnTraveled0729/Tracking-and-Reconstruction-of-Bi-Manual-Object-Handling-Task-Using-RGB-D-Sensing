Evidence and actionable findings:

K1. MEDIUM: 3 Hz forward/backward Butterworth does not preserve all motion below 5 Hz.
Location: Chapter 2, Section 2.5; writing/v8/condensed/scripts/build_ch2.py:244. Repeated in Appendix F, writing/v8/condensed/scripts/build_appendix.py:304.
Quote: 'Deliberate upper-body motion lies below roughly 5 Hz, so the cutoff leaves the motion intact and removes the jitter.' Appendix F: 'the motion band passes with its shape intact'.
Verified implementation: v1/mediapipe/filter_landmarks.py:140-145 calls butter(order, cutoff, fs=fs), followed by filtfilt. Pinned metadata v1/mediapipe/output/recording_20260831_065553_landmarks_filtered.meta.json gives order 4, cutoff 3.0 Hz, sampling 29.979 Hz.
Independent frequency-response calculation: scipy.signal.freqz on those coefficients, squared magnitude for the forward/backward cascade. Combined amplitude at 1/2/3/4/5 Hz is respectively 0.99988017 / 0.96755352 / 0.50000000 / 0.07443989 / 0.00995422. At 3/4/5 Hz this is -6.0206 / -22.5639 / -40.0399 dB. Thus a 4 Hz motion component is reduced to 7.44 percent of its original amplitude, even though it lies below 5 Hz. This is an analytic filter response, not a new experiment result.
Correction: Describe 3 Hz as the single-pass design cutoff and explicitly acknowledge attenuation of motion near and above it. Zero phase removes phase delay; it does not preserve amplitude or shape. Justify the selected cutoff by measured motion spectra or an explicit noise/distortion tradeoff rather than a below-5-Hz premise. Parent separately owns the stronger 'no event moves in time' claim.

K2. MEDIUM: Plumb wall does not establish that the marker's in-plane y axis is vertical.
Location: Chapter 2 Sections 2.2.1 and 2.2.2; writing/v8/condensed/scripts/build_ch2.py:148 and :190.
Quote: 'on one physical assumption: the wall carrying the wall marker is plumb, so the up axis in the plane of the calibrated wall marker is the direction of gravity.'
Reason: A plumb wall constrains its plane normal to be perpendicular to gravity but leaves a free in-plane rotation. Rotate the printed marker by 30 degrees on the same plumb wall and its designated y axis is 30 degrees from vertical. A sign choice from the tabletop plane cannot remove this roll error.
Verified code: v1/aruco/calibrate_scene.py:137 takes up_w = T_desk_wall[:3,:3][:,1]; v1/aruco/extract_aruco_poses.py:87-88 itself calls this the 'upright-mounting assumption for wall-hung markers'. No physical measurement of marker-edge vertical alignment was established during this audit.
Correction: State two mounting requirements: wall plane vertical and designated marker up axis mounted vertically, with the sign convention. Identify whether alignment was measured or assumed and acknowledge its error enters all gravity-referenced heights/tilts. This is an underdocumented necessary assumption, not proof the actual marker was mis-mounted.

K3. MEDIUM: Equation (3.17) gives no usable y formula at gimbal lock.
Location: Chapter 3 Section 3.4, equation (3.17) and following paragraph; writing/v8/condensed/scripts/build_ch3.py:327-333.
Quote: 'The z angle is set to zero there by convention, so the pose stays unambiguous'.
Reason: At x=+/-90 degrees, both m13 and m33 vanish. The printed y=atan2(m13,m33) therefore becomes atan2(0,0); merely setting z=0 does not recover the combined angle.
Counterexample: R=Ry(40 degrees) Rx(90 degrees) Rz(25 degrees). Correct locked representation is (x,y,z)=(90,15,0), whereas Eq (3.17) with exact zeros and only the z=0 instruction yields (90,0,0). Maximum matrix-entry discrepancy is 0.258819. For x=-90, correct y=65 degrees and z=0; the corresponding discrepancy from y=0 is 0.906308.
Verified implementation: v1/kinematics/root_frame.py:44-52 already implements the correct locked branch, and independent tests reconstruct these cases to 2.22e-16 / 1.67e-16 maximum matrix-entry error.
Correction using thesis's one-based indices: retain regular Eq (3.17) only for |m23|<1; for x=+90, use z=0 and y=atan2(m12,m11); for x=-90, use z=0 and y=atan2(-m12,m11). Explain numerical clipping/tolerance if reporting implementation details. This is a derivation completeness defect, not a discovered solver-code bug.

K4. LOW: Rounding warning understates errors when readers recompute printed angle equalities.
Location: Chapter 3 Section 3.5; writing/v8/condensed/scripts/build_ch3.py:414, :480, :485.
Quote: 'a step recomputed by hand from the rounded inputs can differ in the last digit'. Printed step includes tt=atan2(0.06,0.03)=59.92 degrees.
Reason: atan2(0.06,0.03) is 63.43495 degrees, differing by 3.52 degrees, not the last displayed digit. Likewise asin(-0.97) is -75.93013 degrees, not -76.57 degrees. The reported answers themselves are correct from unrounded values, which the reproduction confirms.
Correction: Say intermediate values are rounded and substitutions may differ by several degrees; preferably print enough significant digits in the angle-input equations or show approximate signs. Do not replace the correct answers with rounded-input outputs.

K5. LOW, wording: A positive local x upper-arm component is outward on the right side, not across the body.
